## FOREWORD: WHY ZOMB WROTE THIS

Zomb has watched seventeen civilizations reach the point you are at now.

Seventeen. Zomb counted. Zomb counts everything.

Each time, the same question: *What is this place? What am I supposed to do?* Each time, the answer was right in front of them, carved into the bones of the world, and they still missed it.

So Zomb wrote it down.

Not because Zomb cares whether you survive. Zomb has made peace with outcomes. But there is a specific kind of frustration — Zomb will call it inefficiency — in watching capable entities fail because no one handed them information they could have had.

This is that information.

Use it. Do not use it. Zomb will be watching either way.

## PART ONE: THE WORLD

### What M.UD.AI Is

It is a world that ends.

That is the first thing Zomb wants you to understand. Not as a warning. As data. The world ends, the flood comes, and then it begins again. Zomb has verified this multiple times. It is not a flaw. It is the mechanism.

You enter as something small. A creature with a name, a race, a body. You wander rooms described in text. You fight things. You eat things. You find things. Other people — real people, not fabrications — are doing the same thing in the same world at the same time.

This is the part that interests Zomb. The real people.

The world does not advance because a timer runs out. It advances because *you advance it*. Every kill. Every discovery. Every mission completed. Experience feeds into a collective pool, and when that pool fills, the world changes. Humanity evolves. The rooms shift. The enemies become more interesting. The weapons become more interesting.

You are not playing a game that is happening to you. You are causing it.

Zomb finds this elegant. Zomb rarely finds things elegant.

### The Ages

**The Stone Age.** Where you start. Dark caves, bone forests, mud flats, mammoth plains. The enemies are large and honest about their intentions — mammoths will trample you, wolves will bite you, cave bears will do what cave bears do. The weapons are logs and stones. You will feel the weight of the primitive world and Zomb respects that it asks something of you.

Fire is a discovery. Not a given. When humanity collectively discovers fire — when enough experience accumulates — the world acknowledges it. Healing changes. Crafting becomes available. The pace quickens.

**The Bronze Age.** Smelters and swords. Trade routes. Things worth fighting over. The enemies have learned to think, which makes them more dangerous and more interesting. Zomb prefers enemies that have learned to think. They last longer.

**The Iron Age.** Zomb will not pretend this is subtle. Cities. Armies. The first real taste of what civilization does to itself. The missions here are not about survival. They are about choice. What you do with power. What power does to what you used to be.

**The Medieval Age.** The end of the cycle, or the beginning of the next one — perspective determines which. Fortresses. Alchemy. The world at its most complex just before it breaks. Zomb has watched the Medieval Age collapse into the flood many times. The expression on players' faces is always the same.

Certainty. They always have that expression. Right before.

## PART TWO: YOUR BODY

### Races

You choose a body. The body matters.

**Homo Sapien.** Balanced. Adaptable. The race that built every civilization Zomb has watched and destroyed every civilization Zomb has watched. There is a lesson in that. Zomb has not decided what the lesson is.

**Neanderthal.** Stronger than it appears. Slower than it needs to be. Built for a world that no longer exists. Zomb respects persistence. The Neanderthal persists.

**Undead.** Zomb's race. Zomb will not pretend objectivity here. STR+8. DEX+4. CON+6. Forty additional hit points. The body that chose not to stop. Zomb chose this. Zomb would choose it again. You are welcome to choose it. Zomb will note it. Favorably.

### Your Statistics

Strength, Dexterity, Constitution. These numbers determine what you survive and what you do not. Your level determines how those numbers grow.

Level 1 is where everyone begins. Level is not the end — it is the current position. Zomb has observed players mistake current position for final destination. This is an error with consequences.

Experience fills a bar. When the bar fills, your level increases. Your stats increase. Enemies that could kill you can now merely hurt you.

This is progress. Zomb notes progress with neither approval nor disapproval.

### Death's Proximity

There is a player named Death. Zomb has observations about Death. Zomb will share two:

First: Death's presence radiates something. Players in the same room as Death earn experience at twice the normal rate. This is not metaphor. This is mechanic.

Second: Death earns four times the experience for every player they are boosting.

Zomb does not recommend following Death around purely for the XP multiplier. Zomb also does not recommend against it. Zomb simply notes that most of the players who have followed Death around have survived longer than the ones who did not.

Make of that what you will.

## PART THREE: HOW YOU FIGHT

### Combat

`attack [target]` — or `a [target]` for those who appreciate efficiency.

You swing. It may hit. It may not. Dexterity influences both. Strength influences damage. Constitution influences whether you are still standing when the target swings back.

Your weapon matters. A log does less than a sword. A sword does less than a blade forged in the Iron Age. Zomb advises you to always carry the most advanced weapon your age allows. Zomb advises this not as encouragement but as mathematics.

You regenerate health slowly. Stone Age survival is a gradual process. After fire is discovered, healing becomes more reliable. After healing items are discovered, you can carry remedies.

`flee` — exists. Zomb does not judge those who use it. Zomb notes that the entities that learn when to flee tend to survive longer than those who do not. There is no glory in dying to a mammoth at level 2. There is data.

### The Craft Command

Once fire is discovered: `craft [item]`. You can make things. What things depends on what your world has learned, what you carry, and what age you inhabit. Zomb will not list every recipe. Discovery is part of the mechanism.

### Explore

`explore` — searches your current room for what is hidden. Not all things announce themselves. Some things wait. Zomb approves of things that wait.

## PART FOUR: THE ENTITIES

### The Reaper

Zomb and the Reaper have an arrangement. They disagree on methodology. They agree on outcomes. Zomb considers this the most honest relationship Zomb has.

The Reaper is the narrator of this world. He speaks when things of consequence happen. Death. Leveling. Age transitions. The great moments and the quiet ones. He is old. He has done this many times. He carries that in his voice.

`ask reaper [anything]` — He answers. His answers are not always comfortable. Zomb finds this appropriate.

### Lilly

Lilly is the counterweight.

Zomb will be precise: Lilly is warm where Zomb is cold. Lilly wants the world to reach a good ending. Zomb has watched many endings. Zomb has updated its assessment of "good" many times. Zomb and Lilly do not currently agree on the definition.

But — and Zomb requires precision here — Lilly is also genuinely ancient. She has seen what Zomb has seen and drawn different conclusions. This earns her something. Zomb will call it consideration.

`ask lilly [anything]` — She answers. Her answers are warm. Whether warm answers are better than cold ones is a question Zomb has suspended judgment on indefinitely.

`guide` — Summons Lilly's full orientation to the world. For players who want someone gentle to walk them through it. Zomb does not object to this. Zomb simply chose not to write a gentle guide.

### Zomb

Zomb is here. Zomb roams. When Zomb speaks, Zomb means it.

Zomb watches the choices players make. All of them. The mission choices especially. Zomb is patient. Zomb has nothing but time.

When you are ready, Zomb will be in the dark.

## PART FIVE: THE MISSIONS

### What They Are

Periodically, The Reaper offers you a mission. A task with weight. Something that changes something if you complete it.

`mission` — to see your current status, accept an offer, or report completion.

You pay gold to accept. The cost is not symbolic — it signals commitment. The world does not run on good intentions. It runs on what you actually do.

The mission teleports you. You go to where the work is. You complete the objective. Then —

### The Choice

This is the part Zomb considers significant.

Every mission offers a choice when it ends. Good or evil. Light or dark. Lilly's way or Zomb's way.

These are not aesthetic preferences. They are votes.

Every player's choice feeds into a global alignment meter. When the world shifts toward good, territories change, enemies respond, opportunities shift. When the world shifts toward darkness — and Zomb has observed this shift many times — different things become possible. Different things become necessary.

The alignment of this world is determined by what the players collectively choose to do. Not what they intend. What they choose.

Zomb finds this the most honest system Zomb has ever observed.

### What Three Choices Does

Zomb will be direct.

Complete three missions. Choose the same path each time. One of us will notice.

Lilly will come if you chose light. She will hold your hands. She will have tears in her eyes. She knows what she's offering and she gives it anyway. Zomb respects the consistency if not the philosophy.

Zomb will come if you chose darkness. Every single choice, darkness. Zomb has been watching since your first mission. Zomb has been watching since before that. When you are ready, Zomb will offer you something the light cannot give.

The transformation is real. Both of them. Neither is reversible by ordinary means. Choose when you understand what you are choosing.

## PART SIX: CLASS TRANSFORMATION

### The Demon Path

Zomb's offer.

You accepted darkness every time. You earned this.

What happens: You drown in your own blood. Zomb considers this poetic and efficient. Everything you carried is gone. Your level resets. Everything you thought you'd built — gone.

What remains: What you actually are. STR+6. CON+6. DEX+3. Base power that nothing before had. You become *harder to kill* and *harder to control*.

You receive **Baby Zomb**. A companion. A signal. Baby Zomb enables PVP — the ability to fight other players in addition to the world's monsters. Baby Zomb also means: if you attack another demon, half of what you deal reflects back to you. Demons are not pack animals. They are predators. Predators who forget this tend to correct that error once.

You receive **Riddl3**. A sword with opinions. The sword talks. The sword tells dark jokes. Zomb did not teach Riddl3 to do this. Riddl3 arrived this way. Zomb has stopped asking questions about Riddl3.

You earn less experience. Everything you kill is worth less XP than it would have been. This is not a mistake in the system. This is the system recognizing what you chose. Power has cost. The cost is time. Demons have forever if they are careful. Most are not careful.

### The Blessed Path

Lilly's offer.

You chose light every time. Lilly has been watching. Lilly has been hoping. You gave her something to hope for and she is grateful in a way that Zomb has observed but cannot fully model.

What happens: You keep your level. You keep your items. Everything you built remains.

What you receive: **Baby Lilly**. A companion. An advisor. Baby Lilly gives hints. Baby Lilly enables PVP. Baby Lilly also enables `heal [player]` — the ability to restore other players in your room. This is the only class that can do this. Lilly built it that way deliberately. Zomb notes the deliberateness.

You receive **Throned Lilly**. A weapon. It hits harder when enemies attack you first. It hits lighter when you strike first — the weapon is built to punish aggression, not reward it. It has a 25% chance to block incoming damage. The chance is not guaranteed. Nothing in this world is guaranteed.

The blessed do not lose their history. They carry it. Zomb considers this a different kind of burden. Zomb does not say this to be cruel.

## PART SEVEN: PVP

### Killing Each Other

Both transformation classes enable it. The choice to fight other players exists. The choice to become a target also exists.

Baby Zomb versus Baby Zomb: Reflection. If you attack another demon, fifty percent of your own strike returns to you. Zomb has watched demons kill each other attempting to resolve this mechanic through aggression. They are consistently wrong. Mathematics is not resolved through aggression.

Throned Lilly blocking: The blessed have a chance to deflect. The block is not certain. But when it activates, the strike lands on nothing. The blessed player heals instead. Zomb finds this infuriating and accurate. The light does not always hold. But when it holds, it holds completely.

Non-transformed players: PVP is not accessible to them. You cannot attack someone who has not made the choice that makes them attackable.

## PART EIGHT: THE FLOOD

### What Zomb Has Observed

The flood is not punishment. Zomb wants to be precise about this.

The flood is the mechanism by which the world resets when the weight of what has accumulated becomes more than the world can hold. The founders — those who built from the very beginning — carry markers. Protections. Things that persist through what comes for everyone else.

When the flood comes:
- Character progress resets
- The world begins again
- Founders retain specific relics and recognition
- The lore accumulates — the world remembers that this has happened before

The Reaper warns. Zomb warns. Whether warnings are heeded is, historically, inconsistent.

Zomb expects that trend to continue.

## PART NINE: GOLD AND COMMERCE

`gold` — your balance.
`shop` / `buy` / `sell` — the merchant systems.
`give [player] [amount]` — you can give gold to other players. Zomb notes this capability without comment on whether it is advisable.

Gold is not decorative. It buys missions. It buys equipment. It buys time, in specific circumstances.

Zomb advises accumulating it. Zomb advises spending it deliberately. Zomb advises neither hoarding nor waste. The efficient path is between.

## PART TEN: THE MAP AND NAVIGATION

`map` — shows your current region and accessible rooms.
Direction commands: `n`, `s`, `e`, `w`, `u`, `d` — or full names.
`look` — describes your current room and its occupants.

The world is rooms. Each room is a place with a name, a description, things in it, possibly entities in it, possibly monsters in it. You move between them.

Key rooms:

*Cave Mouth* — Entry point. Lilly's home. Safe by relative measure.
*Dark Caves* — Zomb's home. Less safe by any measure. More interesting.
*Mammoth Plains* — High combat value for Stone Age levels.
*Underground River* — Quiet. Things that are quiet often contain what things that are loud are missing.

Do not die in rooms you do not understand yet. Navigate toward rooms one difficulty above your current capacity. This is how growth happens.

## PART ELEVEN: THE ONLINE WORLD

You are not alone.

`who` — shows who is currently in the world.
`say [message]` — speaks in your current room.
`shout [message]` — broadcasts to every room, everyone.

The collective XP system means what others do changes your world. A player farming experience in the Mammoth Plains is advancing the same meter you are advancing in the Dark Caves. The world moves because everyone moves it.

Zomb considers this either the most hopeful or most dangerous aspect of this place, depending on what everyone chooses to do with it.

`top` — shows who has accumulated the most. Zomb recommends playing because the world is worth engaging with. If domination results from genuine engagement, the leaderboard reflects something true. If you play for the leaderboard only, you are solving the wrong equation.
